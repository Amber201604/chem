# Digestion of a tomato (mouth → stomach)

This note records **real-world facts** that sit behind the 04.1 classroom animation. The app uses oversized ions, cartoon chewing, and a single tomato travelling in one piece; those are teaching models.

## What a tomato actually contains

Typical ripe tomato (by mass) is mostly water (~94%). The rest is:

- simple sugars (glucose, fructose) and some sucrose
- fibre (cell-wall polysaccharides, not digested by human enzymes in the mouth/stomach)
- a small amount of protein
- organic acids, minerals, and pigments such as lycopene

Starch is **low** in a ripe tomato. The animation still shows salivary amylase because that enzyme is the standard mouth example, and because mixed meals often include starch.

## Mouth

- **Mechanical digestion:** teeth increase surface area.
- **Saliva:** water, mucus, electrolytes, salivary amylase (ptyalin), and a little lingual lipase.
- **Amylase** hydrolyses internal α-1,4 bonds in starch → maltose and limit dextrins. It does **not** finish digestion to glucose, and it does not digest protein or fibre.
- Lingual lipase can start fat digestion; a tomato is very low in fat.

## Oesophagus

Peristalsis (sequential circular-muscle contraction) moves the bolus. The upper sphincter opens for swallowing; the lower (cardiac) sphincter opens into the stomach and then closes to limit reflux.

## Stomach: acid, pepsinogen, pepsin

| Cell (gastric mucosa) | Product | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mucous cells | mucus + bicarbonate | physical/chemical barrier so the wall is not digested |
| Parietal cells | H⁺ and Cl⁻ (HCl), intrinsic factor | denature proteins; kill many microbes; activate pepsinogen |
| Chief cells | **pepsinogen**, gastric lipase | pepsinogen is the inactive zymogen of pepsin |

Acid production (parietal cell):

\[
\mathrm{CO_2 + H_2O \rightleftharpoons H_2CO_3 \rightleftharpoons H^+ + HCO_3^-}
\]

H⁺ is pumped into the lumen by H⁺/K⁺-ATPase. Cl⁻ follows. HCO₃⁻ enters blood (alkaline tide).

Activation:

\[
\mathrm{pepsinogen \xrightarrow{HCl\ (and\ pepsin)} pepsin}
\]

Pepsin is an endopeptidase. It cuts proteins into smaller peptides; it does not release a complete set of free amino acids. Optimum pH is strongly acidic (~1.5–2.5).

## Pepsinogen vs trypsinogen (the “trapotaphen” mix-up)

Students sometimes blend **pepsinogen** and **trypsinogen** into one imaginary stomach enzyme.

- **Pepsinogen / pepsin:** stomach; activated by gastric acid (and by pepsin itself).
- **Trypsinogen / trypsin:** pancreas → duodenum; activated by enteropeptidase, then trypsin. It does **not** work with gastric acid; pancreatic juice is alkaline.

## Mucosa cells, enzymes, and respiration

Chief cells **do not store active pepsin**. They synthesise pepsinogen on ribosomes, pack it in zymogen granules, and export it by **exocytosis**.

Fuel and waste inside the cell (same logic as other animal cells):

\[
\mathrm{C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6\,O_2 \rightarrow 6\,CO_2 + 6\,H_2O + ATP}
\]

- **Glucose** (from blood, not directly from the tomato sitting in the lumen) is oxidised in mitochondria.
- **ATP** pays for protein synthesis and for the parietal proton pump.
- **CO₂ and H₂O** and other unused metabolites leave the cell.
- **Amino acids** are assembled into pepsinogen (useful protein for secretion), not chopped up by pepsin inside the cytosol.

Nutrient **absorption** of tomato sugars and peptides is mainly in the **small intestine**, not the gastric lining.

## Teaching model vs reality

| In the app | In the body |
| --- | --- |
| One tomato stays visible as red lumps | Food becomes a mixed chyme |
| H⁺ / Cl⁻ drawn as labelled dots | Ions in solution; no “acid particles” of that size |
| Pepsin drawn as a glowing circle cutting a yellow bar | Enzyme–substrate collisions at molecular scale |
| Mucosa cell “eats” labelled glucose on screen | Glucose arrives via capillaries |
| Journey stops at the stomach | Digestion continues in the small intestine (bile, pancreatic enzymes, brush-border enzymes) |
